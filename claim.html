The idea connecting my work

Central learning claim

Literacy skills translate to more aspects of life than we may realize, and literary analysis is an important part of developing those skills.

My central learning claim

Literacy is not limited to reading a page or writing an essay. It is a collection of skills we use to understand messages, question information, recognize perspective, make connections, and communicate clearly. These skills matter in school, in the workplace, in everyday decisions, and in how we understand the world around us.

Literary analysis is an important part of this learning because it asks us to look closely at how meaning is created. When we examine a text's language, structure, context, and point of view, we practice supporting interpretations with evidence instead of relying only on first impressions.

Synthesis statement

Across my Comp-101 work, I am learning that strong literacy involves more than understanding what a text says. It also means examining how and why it communicates, considering whose perspective is represented, and using evidence to explain my own interpretation. Literary analysis strengthens these habits of close reading and critical thinking. Together, these skills help me become a more thoughtful reader, writer, student, and communicator.
