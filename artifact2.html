Assignment 2

Developing an Interpretive Claim: Writing Process Reflection

Introduction to this artifact

Assignment focus: A writing-process reflection explaining how I developed an interpretive claim, revised a key sentence, and selected and analyzed textual evidence.

Why I selected it: This piece makes the thinking behind my literary analysis visible. It documents how I formed my comparison of Blanco and Hughes, reconsidered wording, and chose a quotation that helped me explain Blanco’s vision of the river.

What I learned: I learned that interpretation develops through revision. A strong claim and a useful quotation do more than summarize a text; they help a reader understand and evaluate an argument.

Complete assignment

My interpretive claim is “The poems *Complaint of El rio grande* by Richard Blanco and *The Negro Speaks of Rivers* by Langston Hughes both make use of rivers as central metaphors in order to examine cultures, communities, and history. Even though both poets deal with these themes, they do so from different perspectives.” I made this claim based on the observation of both speakers tone, language, and use of symbolism saying things like “I was meant for all things to meet” or “my soul has grown deep like rivers.” These quotes developed my observations and both connect the reader and explain communities, and history.

“Rivers always flow and carry traces of different communities to one another, but they can also divide those same communities if people allow it to do so.” I revised this sentence five times. It started as, “Rivers carry traces of different communities but they also divide them.” But I felt as though I could dive deeper into the sentence and really draw out both poets' sides. So I added “Rivers flow and carry traces”; I feel like this amplifies Hughes's side. And then I wanted to dive into Blanco's side, so I added “But they can also divide those same communities if people allow it to do so.” This exemplifies Blanco's claim that the American communities used the Rio Grande as a geographical border instead of a barrier.

A quotation I selected is "turn eons of loveless rocks into lovesick pebbles and carry them as humble gifts back to the sea which brings life back to me" (lines 5-7). This is a quote from Blanco's poem. I selected this quote because it shows what Blanco wanted the Rio Grande to be and what it should’ve been. This quotation allowed me to explore the literal movement of the river as well as the symbolic movement of history and culture. The key words in this to me are “carry them as humble gifts.” I interpret this as rivers are supposed to take things as small as items to things greater than that, like history, and carry them to new locations. The image he provides, “turn eons of loveless rocks into lovesick pebbles,” shows how rivers move and change things into love-filled gifts, making them great things that are filled with love and history. This quote supports my thesis by showing that rivers should carry history through them. Blanco uses the word “eons,” which implies rivers work over long periods of time, capturing eons of history and carrying that through them.

Another person can interpret this quote as if Blanco is speaking about how the Rio Grande actually does connect everything and brings life to people, but that interpretation is wrong. Blanco is speaking about how he thinks the river should perform, because it should connect things, not separate them and act as a barrier. You can see this by reading the rest of the passage. I can see why another quote could support my thesis better, but I really enjoy the way Blanco speaks on what he wants the Rio Grande to be. It adds so much emphasis to his complaint, making the entire poem more powerful. This quotation helps show how Blanco imagines the river as something capable of healing and uniting, not dividing things. The image also emphasizes that the river works and flows over a long period of time, shaping identity, history, and culture. It shows the river acting as a giver, a carrier, and something that transforms life and history—which is everything the river isn’t because it is being used as a border and a blockade.
